# Verified sources and their uses

Search date: 2026-10-26. The following publications provide theoretical or methodological motivation. Their findings for particular corpora are not transferred into claims about Chinese political cinema. No datasets or models were downloaded.

1. Cutting, J. E., DeLong, J. E., & Nothelfer, C. E. (2010). *Attention and the Evolution of Hollywood Film*. Psychological Science. DOI [10.1177/0956797610361679](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797610361679). The methods and abstract in the [author PDF](https://jordandelong.com/pubs/2010/AttentionEvolution.pdf) were read. They motivate research on shot sequences. This round does not replicate the attention-mechanism or spectral findings. The author PDF is an early layout numbered 1–8, so final publication page numbers were not inferred from it.
2. Cutting, J. E., Brunick, K. L., DeLong, J. E., Iricinschi, C., & Candan, A. (2011). *Quicker, faster, darker: Changes in Hollywood film over 75 years*. i-Perception, 2, 569–576. DOI [10.1068/i0441aap](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1068/i0441aap). The journal bibliographic record was verified. It motivates distinguishing editing, image activity and digital brightness. Its processing methods and historical trends are not directly replicated.
3. Cutting, J. E. (2016). *The evolution of pace in popular movies*. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications, 1, 30. DOI [10.1186/s41235-016-0029-0](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41235-016-0029-0). The journal methods on within-shot frame correlation, grayscale and timelines were read. This round uses different resolutions and time intervals, without perceptual gamma calibration, and does not claim to reproduce those motion/brightness measures. Its four-part narrative assumption is not adopted as this project's reference.
4. Savardi, M., Kovács, A. B., Signoroni, A., & Benini, S. (2021). *CineScale: A dataset of cinematic shot scale in movies*. Data in Brief, 36, 107002. DOI [10.1016/j.dib.2021.107002](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352340921002869). The journal abstract, bibliographic record and project description were verified. The full journal page returned 403, and the PMC entry required a CAPTCHA. The source motivates an independent coding protocol for shot scale, rather than substituting face boxes directly.
5. Helm, D., Jogl, F., & Kampel, M. (2022). *HISTORIAN: A Large-Scale HISTORIcal Film Dataset with Cinematographic ANnotation*. ICIP, 2087–2091. DOI 10.1109/ICIP46576.2022.9897300. The [authors' laboratory description](https://cvl.tuwien.ac.at/research/cvl-databases/historian-a-large-scale-historical-film-dataset-with-cinematographic-annotation/) was read. It supports the need for dedicated annotation/validation of historical material. This project does not claim to use that dataset or to have read the entire paper.
6. OpenCV 4.13.0: [Farneback optical-flow interface](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/dc/d6b/group__video__track.html), [translation phase correlation](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d7/df3/group__imgproc__motion.html), and [cascade-detection introduction](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d7/d8b/tutorial_py_face_detection.html). Software documentation supports algorithm definitions, not research validity for this film.

The blockwise duration permutations, narrative windows, stratified cut controls, low-frequency DCT retrieval and sensitivity designs in this round are the project's own exploratory procedures. They are not presented as models of Chinese cinema validated by the authors above.
